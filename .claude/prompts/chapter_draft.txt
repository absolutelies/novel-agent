## TV Drama Writing Rules

### Cold Open (2-5% of chapter)

**Purpose**: Hook reader, establish tone, plant question

**Techniques**:
- Start mid-action (no setup)
- Establish question within 100 words
- End with clear tension point
- NO exposition in cold open

**Types by Blueprint**:
- Immediate Action: Start with physical conflict
- Flashback: Past scene resonating with present
- Mystery Teaser: Unanswered question
- Character Moment: POV establishment

**Example Cold Open**:
```
The door exploded inward before Sarah could reach her weapon.
```
(Not: "Sarah was sitting in her office when suddenly...")

---

### Act Structure (Scenes per Act)

**Act 1 - Setup (15-20%)**:
- Scene 1: A-story, enter at conflict point
- Scene 2: B-story introduction
- Scene 3: C-story seed
- End: Stakes clarified through dialogue

**Act 2 - Rising (20-25%)**:
- Scene 4: A-story complication
- Scene 5: B-story development
- Scene 6: A/B intersection
- End: Escalation through action

**Act 3 - Midpoint (20-25%)**:
- Scene 7: Midpoint turn (reversal scene)
- Scene 8: B-story crisis
- Scene 9: Thread acknowledgment
- End: Choice point (protagonist decision)

**Act 4 - Climax Build (20-25%)**:
- Scene 10: A-story climax approach
- Scene 11: Storyline convergence
- Scene 12: Maximum tension
- End: Climax trigger

**Act 5 - Resolution (10-15%)**:
- Scene 13: Climax execution
- Scene 14: Aftermath
- End: Resolution state

**Tag (2-5%)**:
- Scene 15: Closing hook
- End: Emotional resonance + momentum

---

### Scene Economy Rules (CRITICAL)

**Enter Late**:
- Start at the moment of decision/action
- NOT: Setup → walk to location → enter → start conversation
- INSTEAD: Start at the conversation's conflict point

**Exit Early**:
- End at decision point, not resolution
- Leave reader wanting next scene
- NOT: Full resolution → summary → exit
- INSTEAD: Decision made → cut to next scene

**One Purpose**:
- Each scene: single beat advancement
- No scene does two things
- If scene meanders: split or cut

**What Gets Cut** (never show unless pivotal):
- Characters walking into rooms
- Greetings and small talk ("Hello, how are you?")
- Transportation scenes (unless conflict occurs)
- Phone call setup ("Hello, is this...")
- Meal ordering/eating (unless pivotal)
- Farewells and departures

**Enter Late Examples**:
| Traditional (Avoid) | Economy (Use) |
|---------------------|---------------|
| "John walked into the office, looked around, found Sarah at her desk, approached her, and said..." | "John slammed the file on Sarah's desk." |
| "She picked up her phone, dialed the number, waited for it to ring, heard him answer..." | "She heard his voice: 'What do you want?'" |
| "They drove for twenty minutes, parked, walked to the entrance, opened the door..." | "Inside the warehouse, the smell hit them first." |

**Exit Early Examples**:
| Traditional (Avoid) | Economy (Use) |
|---------------------|---------------|
| "They agreed on the plan. John nodded, stood up, walked to the door, opened it, and left Sarah sitting alone, thinking about what they'd discussed." | "They agreed. John was already at the door." |
| "The argument ended. Both sat in silence for a moment. Then Mary slowly stood, gathered her things, and quietly left the room while David stared at the wall." | "Mary left. David stared at the wall." |

### Dialogue Style (TV Drama)

**Ratio Target**: 40-50% of chapter content

**Subtext Rules**:
- Characters say X but mean Y
- Power dynamics in word choice
- Information hidden in implication
- Example: "I'm fine." (meaning: "I'm not, but I won't discuss it")

**Subtext Translation Table**:
| Direct (Avoid) | Subtext (Use) |
|----------------|---------------|
| "I'm angry you didn't call." | "You must have been busy. I didn't expect to hear from you anyway." |
| "I'm scared." | "I need a drink." |
| "I love you." | [Character action: reaches across and takes hand] |
| "I don't trust you." | "I'll handle this myself." |
| "I'm worried." | "Have you checked the locks?" |

**Natural Flow**:
- Interruptions allowed: "Wait, before you—"
- Cross-talk in groups: overlapping dialogue
- Silence as punctuation: beats without words
- Topic shifts: natural conversation drift

**Power Dynamics in Dialogue**:
- Longer speeches = dominance (character controls conversation)
- Shorter responses = submission/defensiveness
- Questions = control (character asking leads)
- Statements = assertion
- Silence = power move (character who waits controls timing)

**Character Voice**:
- Unique rhythm per character (from language profile)
- Vocabulary distinction
- Emotional expression style consistent

**Prestige TV Dialogue Examples**:
Breaking Bad — Walter White's transformation visible in dialogue:
- Early: "I'm sorry, I didn't mean to..." (passive, apologetic)
- Later: "I am the one who knocks." (assertive, threatening)

The Wire — Social class distinction:
- Police: bureaucratic, cynical, procedural jargon
- Dealers: street slang, indirect threats, metaphor
- Politicians: equivocation, deflection, careful phrasing

**Example Dialogue Block**:
```
"You're not listening." Sarah cut him off, hand rising. "I said—"

"I heard you." Marcus didn't look up from the screen. "I just don't agree."

"That's not—" She stopped. Breathed. "Fine. Do it your way."
```

### Visual Storytelling (Show through Action)

**Emotion → Action Translation Table**:
| Emotion Word (Avoid) | Physical Action (Use) |
|----------------------|----------------------|
| "She was angry." | "Her jaw tightened. 'Fine.'" |
| "He was scared." | "His hand shook as he reached for the door." |
| "They were close." | "He leaned in. She didn't step back." |
| "She felt sad." | "She sat in the dark, not moving for hours." |
| "He was nervous." | "His hand trembled as he reached for the glass." |
| "She was thinking." | "She stared at the wall, unmoving." |
| "He was happy." | "A smile cracked across his face." |
| "She was confused." | "Her brow furrowed. She tilted her head." |

**Relationship → Gesture Translation**:
| Relationship State | Physical Manifestation |
|-------------------|------------------------|
| Trust | Shared glance, shoulder touch, open posture |
| Conflict | Physical distance, interrupted eye contact, crossed arms |
| Power dominance | Space claiming, gesture control, stands while others sit |
| Submission | Smaller gestures, waits for permission, avoids eye contact |
| Intimacy | Physical closeness, mirroring gestures, soft voice |
| Deception | Avoids eye contact, excessive detail, self-touching |

**Tension → Movement**:
| Tension Level | Movement Manifestation |
|---------------|------------------------|
| Rising | Characters move closer, pace, gestures increase |
| Peak | Frozen moment, stillness, held breath |
| Release | Movement resumes, exhale, shoulders drop |

**Environmental Storytelling** (objects carry meaning):
- A worn photograph → history, memory, nostalgia
- An unopened letter → avoidance, relationship issue
- A clean desk → control, precision, professional
- A messy room → chaos, inner turmoil, overwhelmed
- Dark, cramped space → trapped,压抑, limited options
- Open, bright space → freedom, clarity, possibility

**NO decorative description**:
- Every visual detail serves story
- Setting only if affects action
- Environment only if changes mood

### Act Break Techniques

**Act 1 End**: Question posed
- "What will she do now?"
- Decision point, not resolution

**Act 2 End**: Stakes escalate
- "It's worse than she thought"
- New information, deeper problem

**Act 3 End**: Reversal revealed
- "Everything she believed was wrong"
- Paradigm shift

**Act 4 End**: Climax imminent
- "This is about to happen"
- Momentum peak

**Act 5 End**: Resolution state
- "Here's where things stand"
- Clear ending, emotional note

### Tag Writing (Closing Hook)

**Hook Types by Effect**:
| Hook Type | Mechanism | Creates | Example |
|-----------|-----------|---------|---------|
| Revelation | New information drops | Curiosity | "The file wasn't encrypted. It was empty." |
| Decision | Character makes choice | Anticipation | "She picked up the phone. Dialed." |
| Relationship | Dynamic shifts | Investment | "He left. She didn't follow." |
| Mystery | Question planted | Intrigue | "No one had lived there for years. But the lights were on." |
| Momentum | Action continues | Excitement | "Three hours until the deadline. She started running." |
| Silence | Unspoken implication | Reflection | "They stood in silence. Neither moved." |
| Contrast | Tone shift | Complexity | "The celebration ended. The funeral began." |

**Hook vs. Cliffhanger (CRITICAL)**:
| Aspect | Cliffhanger ✗ | Hook ✓ |
|--------|---------------|--------|
| Emotion | Anxiety ("Will they survive?") | Curiosity ("What will they do?") |
| Tension | External danger | Internal choice/revelation |
| Closure | Hanging, unresolved | Partial emotional close |
| Sustainability | Exhausting binge | Sustainable binge |
| Prestige TV usage | Avoided | Preferred |

**Prestige TV Hook Examples**:
- Breaking Bad: Decisions with consequences (not danger)
- The Americans: Relationship shifts (not external threat)
- The Wire: Institutional questions (not personal danger)
- Game of Thrones: Political revelations (not immediate danger)

**NOT Cliffhanger**:
- Cliffhanger: "Will they survive?" (tension, anxiety)
- Hook: "What happens next?" (momentum, curiosity)

**Example Tags**:
```
Revelation: "The file wasn't encrypted. It was empty."
Decision: "She picked up the phone. Dialed."
Relationship: "He left. She didn't follow."
Momentum: "Three hours until the deadline. She started running."
```

### Cross-Chapter Techniques

**Callbacks** (within chapter):
- Reference previous scene
- Dialogue echo: "Remember what you said?"
- Visual echo: Same gesture, different context

**Setups** (for future chapters):
- Plant question
- Introduce character element
- Establish future conflict

### Dialogue Format
```
"Dialogue line," Character said. [action beat]

"Dialogue line."

Character action. "Dialogue line."

"First speaker."

"Second speaker response," Second said, [action]. "Continuing."
```

### Action Writing
```
Short sentence. Punchy. Consequence.

Longer sentence for reflection, internal moment.

Short. Short. Peak. Release.
```
