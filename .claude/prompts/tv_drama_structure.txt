## Research Foundation

This structure is based on analysis of HBO/FX prestige dramas:
- **The Wire**: Novelistic, sociological, "visual novel" structure
- **Breaking Bad**: Classical tragedy, transformation arc, escalation pacing
- **The Americans**: Hybrid thriller + family drama, relationship hooks
- **Game of Thrones**: Epic multi-POV, convergence structure
- **Mad Men**: Character-focused, distributed pacing

**Key Insight**: Each season = One novel. Each episode = One chapter.
Streaming era prefers hooks (curiosity) over cliffhangers (anxiety).

## Modern American TV Drama Structure

### Episode Components

| Component | Description | Timing |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **Cold Open** | Opening scene before title/credits | 2-5 min |
| **Act 1** | Setup after cold open | 10-15 min |
| **Act 2** | Rising action | 10-15 min |
| **Act 3** | Midpoint/complications | 10-15 min |
| **Act 4** | Climax building | 10-15 min |
| **Act 5** | Resolution + Tag | 5-10 min |
| **Tag** | Final scene (hook for next) | 1-3 min |

### Storyline Structure (A/B/C)

**A-Story** (Primary, 60-70% screen time):
- Main plot driving episode
- Central conflict and stakes
- Protagonist's primary arc movement

**B-Story** (Secondary, 20-30% screen time):
- Supporting character arc
- Parallel theme exploration
- Often intersects with A-story

**C-Story** (Tertiary, 5-15% screen time):
- Setup/payoff for future episodes
- Comic relief or contrast
- World-building elements

## Act Break Rules (Binge-Optimized)

### Traditional Network (with commercials)
- Act 1 break: 15 min mark
- Act 2 break: 30 min mark
- Act 3 break: 45 min mark
- Each break = mini-cliffhanger

### Streaming/Binge Era (Netflix, HBO, Amazon)
- No commercial breaks
- Episodes end with **hooks** (not cliffhangers)
- "What happens next?" vs "Will they survive?"
- Emotional/thematic resonance > plot tension

### Hook vs. Cliffhanger (CRITICAL)

| Aspect | Cliffhanger ✗ (Avoid) | Hook ✓ (Use) |
|--------|----------------------|--------------|
| Emotion | Anxiety, fear | Curiosity, interest |
| Question | "Will they survive?" | "What will they do?" |
| Tension | External danger | Internal choice |
| Closure | None (hanging) | Partial (emotional close) |
| Binge effect | Exhausting | Sustainable |

**Hook Types**:
- Revelation hook: New information reframes situation
- Decision hook: Character makes consequential choice
- Relationship hook: Dynamic between characters shifts
- Mystery hook: Question planted for future
- Momentum hook: Action continues without pause
- Silence hook: Unspoken moment with implication
- Contrast hook: Tone shift creates complexity

**Prestige TV Examples**:
- Breaking Bad: Decisions (not danger) as episode endings
- The Americans: Relationship shifts as hooks
- The Wire: Institutional questions planted
- Game of Thrones: Political revelations

## Narrative Beats per Episode

### Cold Open Beat (Opening Hook)
- Establish tone
- Plant question or tension
- Time: 100-300 words (novel equivalent)

### Act 1 Beats (Setup)
- Scene 1: A-story establishment
- Scene 2: B-story introduction
- Scene 3: C-story seed
- **End beat**: Question/stakes clarified
- Time: 500-800 words per "scene"

### Act 2 Beats (Rising)
- Scene 4: A-story complication
- Scene 5: B-story development
- Scene 6: A/B intersection
- **End beat**: Stakes escalate
- Time: 500-800 words per "scene"

### Act 3 Beats (Midpoint)
- Scene 7: Midpoint turn (reversal or revelation)
- Scene 8: B-story crisis point
- Scene 9: C-story payoff or setup
- **End beat**: Protagonist faces harder choice
- Time: 500-800 words per "scene"

### Act 4 Beats (Climax Building)
- Scene 10: A-story climax approach
- Scene 11: All storylines converge
- Scene 12: Maximum tension
- **End beat**: Climax imminent
- Time: 500-800 words per "scene"

### Act 5 Beats (Resolution)
- Scene 13: Climax execution
- Scene 14: Immediate aftermath
- **End beat**: Resolution complete
- Time: 500-800 words per "scene"

### Tag Beat (Closing Hook)
- Single scene
- Emotional resonance
- Plants next episode question
- Time: 100-300 words

## Visual Storytelling Rules (TV Style)

**Scene Economy** (TV constraint):
- Each scene = one clear purpose
- Enter late, exit early
- No meandering setup

**What Gets Cut** (never show unless pivotal):
- Characters walking into rooms
- Greetings and small talk
- Transportation scenes (unless conflict)
- Phone call setup ("Hello, is this...")
- Meal ordering/eating (unless pivotal)
- Farewells and departures

**Enter Late Examples**:
| Traditional (Avoid) | Economy (Use) |
|---------------------|---------------|
| "John walked into the office, looked around, found Sarah..." | "John slammed the file on Sarah's desk." |
| "She picked up her phone, dialed, waited..." | "She heard his voice: 'What do you want?'" |

**Exit Early Examples**:
| Traditional (Avoid) | Economy (Use) |
|---------------------|---------------|
| "They agreed. John nodded, stood, walked to the door, opened it, and left..." | "They agreed. John was already at the door." |
| "The argument ended. Mary stood, gathered things, quietly left..." | "Mary left. David stared at the wall." |

**Show through action**:
| Avoid (Telling) | Use (Showing) |
|----------------|---------------|
| "He was nervous." | "His hand trembled as he reached for the glass." |
| "She felt angry." | "She slammed the folder onto the desk." |
| "He was thinking." | "He stared at the wall, unmoving." |
| "She was sad." | "She sat in the dark for hours." |
| "They loved each other." | "He reached across and took her hand." |

**Environmental Storytelling**:
- Objects carry meaning (worn photo → memory, unopened letter → avoidance)
- Spaces reflect character (clean desk → control, messy room → chaos)
- Environment signals mood (dark/cramped → trapped, open/bright → freedom)

**Dialogue rules**:
- Subtext preferred (say one thing, mean another)
- Interruptions natural
- Cross-talk in groups
- Silence as punctuation

## Pacing Techniques

### Micro-Pacing (Scene Level)
- Sentence length: vary for rhythm
- Paragraph length: action short, reflection long
- Dialogue ratio: TV dramas ~40-50% dialogue
- Description: visual, economical

### Macro-Pacing (Episode Level)
- Tension curve: rise → peak → dip → rise
- Emotional variety: alternate intensity types
- Storyline balance: A/B/C rhythm
- Breathing room: buffer moments between peaks

### Season Arc Pacing (Multi-Episode/Chapter)

**Novel-as-Season Model** (from prestige TV analysis):
- Each "season" = 10-15 chapters (novel arc)
- Each chapter = episode (chapter arc)
- Full novel = multiple "seasons" or one complete season

**Season Structure**:
| Chapters | Phase | Function |
|----------|-------|----------|
| 1-2 | Premiere | Setup + inciting incident + strong hooks |
| 3-5 | Rising | Stakes clarification + B-story introduction |
| 6-7 | Pivot | Reversal + revelation + stakes escalate |
| 8-10 | Climax Build | Convergence + countdown feel |
| 11-12 | Resolution | Outcome + transformation |
| 13+ | Finale | Aftermath + seeds + closure |

**Mid-Season Pivot (Chapters 6-7) — Critical Point**:
- Must include reversal or revelation
- Stakes must escalate beyond previous level
- Often changes direction of entire arc
- Examples:
  - Breaking Bad S1E7: Cancer remission traps Walt in crime
  - The Americans: Discovery threatens cover
  - Game of Thrones: Major character death

## TV Drama Chapter Structure (Novel Adaptation)

Each chapter = one "episode" with:

```
COLD OPEN (2-5% of chapter):
- Hook scene
- Establish tone/question

ACT 1 - SETUP (15-20%):
- A-story: Main plot establishment
- B-story: Secondary arc introduction
- C-story: World/character seed

ACT 2 - RISING (20-25%):
- A-story: Complications
- B-story: Development
- Storylines begin intersecting

ACT 3 - MIDPOINT (20-25%):
- A-story: Midpoint turn
- B-story: Crisis
- Revelation or reversal

ACT 4 - CLIMAX BUILD (20-25%):
- A-story: Approaching climax
- All storylines converge
- Maximum tension

ACT 5 - RESOLUTION (10-15%):
- A-story: Climax execution
- Immediate aftermath

TAG (2-5%):
- Closing scene
- Hook for next chapter
- Emotional/thematic resonance
```

## Cross-Episode Techniques

**Three-Chapter Summary Injection**:
Before writing chapter N, generate summary from N-3, N-2, N-1:
- Inherited elements: Active conflicts, unresolved questions, character states
- Adjustable elements: Secondary characters, relationships, world expansion
- Warning markers: Check for planted threads not reinforced, timeline gaps

**Callbacks**:
- Reference previous episodes
- Visual or dialogue echoes
- "Previously on..." equivalent (subtle)
- Same gesture in different context

**Setup-Payoff**:
- Plant in chapter N, payoff in chapter N+M
- Short-term (M=1-2): Scene-level payoff
- Medium-term (M=3-5): Episode-level payoff
- Long-term (M=6+): Season-level payoff

**Running Threads**:
- A-thread: Primary arc (episode to episode)
- B-thread: Secondary arc (3-5 episode span)
- C-thread: Season arc (full season resolution)

## Dialogue Style (TV Drama)

**Character Voice Distinction**:
- Each character: unique speech pattern
- Vocals: rhythm, vocabulary, interrupt style
- Emotional range: how each shows feelings

**Subtext Rules**:
| Direct (Avoid) | Subtext (Use) |
|----------------|---------------|
| "I'm angry you didn't call." | "You must have been busy. I didn't expect to hear from you anyway." |
| "I'm scared." | "I need a drink." |
| "I don't trust you." | "I'll handle this myself." |
| "I'm worried." | "Have you checked the locks?" |

**Power Dynamics in Dialogue**:
- Longer speeches = dominance (controls conversation)
- Shorter responses = submission/defensiveness
- Questions = control (who asks leads)
- Silence = power move (who waits controls timing)

**Conversation Flow**:
- Natural interruptions
- Topic shifts
- Power exchanges
- Silence placement

**Prestige TV Examples**:
- Breaking Bad: Walter's dialogue transforms from passive to dominant
- The Wire: Social class distinction in speech patterns
- The Americans: Subtext-heavy marriage conversations

## Requirements for Framework

1. **Chapter = Episode structure** (cold open → acts → tag)
2. **Multiple storylines** (A/B/C per chapter)
3. **Act-based beats** (5-act structure)
4. **Hook endings** (not cliffhangers, binge-friendly)
5. **Visual storytelling** (economical, show-through-action)
6. **Dialogue-heavy** (40-50% ratio)
7. **Cross-chapter threads** (callbacks, setups, three-chapter summary)
8. **Season arc awareness** (position in novel-as-season)
9. **Scene economy** (enter late, exit early, no meandering)
10. **Subtext in dialogue** (say X, mean Y)
