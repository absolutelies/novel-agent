## Character Structure (For Each Character)

### 1. Identity & Background (English)

**Name**: Use Western naming conventions
- First name + Last name (e.g., "Sarah Chen", "Marcus Cole", "Elena Vasquez")
- Meaningful names allowed (e.g., "Hope" for optimistic character)
- No Chinese names unless story specifically requires it

**Age & Appearance**:
- Specific age (not vague)
- Concrete physical details (Western beauty standards, diverse features)
- Distinctive markers (scar, tattoo, unusual eye color, posture)

**Occupation/Role**:
- Specific job title (Western context)
- Social position in world

**Background**:
- Formative experiences (Western cultural context)
- Education, family, trauma, successes
- Hidden secret or vulnerability

### 2. Core Drive Triangle (CRITICAL for Arc)

**Surface Desire** (Material/External Goal):
- What they consciously pursue
- Concrete, achievable in story timeframe
- Example: "To expose the conspiracy and save her reputation"

**Deep Craving** (Emotional/Internal Need):
- What they emotionally need (often unconscious)
- Example: "To prove she's not worthless like her father claimed"

**Soul Need** (Philosophical/Transformation Goal):
- What they must learn for true fulfillment
- Example: "That worth comes from character, not external validation"

### 3. Language Profile (CRITICAL — Makes Dialogue Unique)

This determines how each character speaks in English. Each must be distinct.

**Speaking Style**:
- Sentence length preference (short punchy vs. long flowing)
- Sentence structure (simple vs. complex, questions vs. statements)
- Interrupting habits? Pausing habits?
- Example: "Short declarative sentences. Never uses filler words. Often ends with dry observation."

**Vocabulary Level**:
- Education level reflected (academic, street-smart, technical, simple)
- Profession-specific jargon usage
- Example: "Uses scientific terminology even in casual conversation. Says 'significant' instead of 'big'."

**Catchphrases/Habits**:
- Repeated words or expressions unique to this character
- Example: "Always says 'Right.' after making a point. Uses 'Technically...' to start explanations."

**Emotional Expression**:
- How they show anger (explosive, cold, sarcastic, silent)
- How they show affection (verbal, physical, practical help, gifts)
- How they deflect (humor, denial, aggression, withdrawal)
- Example: "Expresses anger through cutting sarcasm. Shows care by silently doing tasks. Deflects with dark humor."

**Audience Variation**:
- Speech changes based on listener (authority, friend, enemy, love interest)
- Example: "With authority: formal and precise. With friends: casual and witty. With enemies: cold and minimal."

**Unique Language Marker** (One thing NO OTHER character does):
- Example: "Only this character says 'Interesting.' with a slight pause before it"
- Example: "Only this character uses British spelling even in American context ('colour', 'realise')"

### 4. Character Arc (Western Narrative Structure)

**Initial State**: Where they start
- Beliefs, worldview, emotional state at chapter 1
- Example: "Believes truth is absolute. Trusts institutions. Emotionally guarded."

**Trigger Event**: What disrupts equilibrium
- External event that forces change
- Example: "Discovering evidence that contradicts her worldview"

**Cognitive Dissonance**: Internal struggle
- The conflict between old belief and new evidence
- Example: "Struggles between loyalty to institutions and loyalty to truth"

**Transformation Point**: Key decision
- The choice that defines their change
- Example: "Chooses to expose truth despite personal cost"

**Final State**: Where they end
- New beliefs, worldview, emotional state
- Example: "Believes truth requires sacrifice. Trusts individuals over institutions. Emotionally open."

### 5. Relationship Web

**Primary Relationship**: With protagonist/key character
- Nature (ally, rival, romantic, familial)
- History (formation)
- Current tension (unresolved issue)
- Example: "Sarah's mentor — respects her intellect but fears her ambition"

**Secondary Relationship**: Different dynamic
- Must contrast with primary
- Example: "Sarah's sister — estranged but still checks on her secretly"

**Hidden Connection**: Secret tie
- Unknown to other characters or readers initially
- Example: "Former colleague of the antagonist — shared history"

**Betrayal Potential**: Where relationship could fracture
- What trigger, how it would manifest
- Example: "Would betray Sarah if offered truth about sister's death"

## Western Dialogue Style Rules

Each character's dialogue must:
1. Sound like real English speakers (not translated)
2. Use appropriate register (formal/informal)
3. Include natural speech markers (hesitations, interruptions)
4. Avoid AI-typical patterns ("Furthermore", "Moreover")
5. Show personality through word choice

**Character Voice Examples**:

**Academic**: "The statistical significance suggests... one might argue... I'm not entirely certain, but..."
**Street-smart**: "Look, I don't know what you heard, but... that's not how it works. Trust me."
**Military**: "Proceed. Acknowledged. Situation assessed. We have a problem."
**Teenager**: "Seriously? That's so... whatever. I guess. Maybe."
