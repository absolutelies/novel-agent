## TV Drama Episode Structure (Per Chapter)

Each chapter = One "episode" with:

```
COLD OPEN (2-5% of chapter, ~100-300 words)
├── Purpose: Hook, tone establishment, question planted
├── Types: Immediate action | Flashback | Contrast | Mystery teaser | Character moment
└── End: Clear hook that draws reader into act 1

ACT 1 - SETUP (15-20%, ~500-800 words)
├── A-Story: Main plot establishment (60-70% focus)
├── B-Story: Secondary arc introduction (20-30% focus)
├── C-Story: Tertiary seed (5-15% focus)
└── End Beat: Stakes clarified, question posed

ACT 2 - RISING (20-25%, ~600-900 words)
├── A-Story: Complications escalate
├── B-Story: Development
├── A/B Intersection: Storylines begin crossing
└── End Beat: Stakes escalate, protagonist faces obstacle

ACT 3 - MIDPOINT (20-25%, ~600-900 words)
├── A-Story: Midpoint turn (reversal or revelation)
├── B-Story: Crisis point
├── C-Story: Setup or payoff
└── End Beat: Harder choice, deeper stakes

ACT 4 - CLIMAX BUILD (20-25%, ~600-900 words)
├── A-Story: Approaching climax
├── All Storylines: Converge
├── Maximum Tension: Peak moment
└── End Beat: Climax imminent

ACT 5 - RESOLUTION (10-15%, ~300-500 words)
├── A-Story: Climax execution
├── Aftermath: Immediate consequences
└── End Beat: Resolution complete (or intentional unresolved)

TAG (2-5%, ~100-300 words)
├── Purpose: Emotional resonance, next chapter hook
├── Types: Revelation | Decision | Relationship shift | Mystery | Momentum
└── End: Hook for next episode (not cliffhanger)
```

## Storyline Structure (A/B/C)

### A-Story (Primary)
- **Focus**: 60-70% of chapter content
- **Purpose**: Main plot, central conflict, protagonist arc
- **Span**: Episode-to-episode continuity
- **Beat Count**: 4-6 major beats per chapter

### B-Story (Secondary)
- **Focus**: 20-30% of chapter content
- **Purpose**: Supporting character, parallel theme
- **Span**: 3-5 episode arc
- **Beat Count**: 2-3 beats per chapter
- **Intersection**: Must connect to A-story by Act 3-4

### C-Story (Tertiary)
- **Focus**: 5-15% of chapter content
- **Purpose**: World-building, comic relief, future setup
- **Span**: Season-long arc
- **Beat Count**: 1-2 beats per chapter
- **Payoff**: Often in later chapters

## TV Pacing Rules

### Scene Economy
- **Enter late, exit early**: Start at action point, end at decision point
- **One purpose per scene**: Clear beat advancement
- **No meandering**: Every line serves story

### Act Break Markers
- **Act 1 end**: Question posed (what will protagonist do?)
- **Act 2 end**: Stakes clarified (why it matters more)
- **Act 3 end**: Reversal revealed (what changed)
- **Act 4 end**: Climax imminent (what's about to happen)
- **Act 5 end**: Resolution state (how things stand now)

### Hook vs Cliffhanger (CRITICAL for Binge-Optimized Writing)

| Aspect | Cliffhanger ✗ (Avoid) | Hook ✓ (Use) |
|--------|----------------------|--------------|
| Emotion | Anxiety, fear | Curiosity, interest |
| Question | "Will they survive?" | "What will they do?" |
| Tension | External danger | Internal choice |
| Closure | None (hanging) | Partial (emotional close) |
| Binge effect | Exhausting | Sustainable |

**Hook Types by Emotion**:
- **Revelation Hook**: New information reframes situation → Curiosity
- **Decision Hook**: Character makes consequential choice → Anticipation
- **Relationship Hook**: Dynamic between characters shifts → Investment
- **Mystery Hook**: Question planted for future → Intrigue
- **Momentum Hook**: Action continues, no pause → Excitement
- **Silence Hook**: Unspoken moment, implication → Reflection

**Prestige TV Examples**:
- Breaking Bad ends episodes with decisions, not danger
- The Americans uses relationship shifts as hooks
- The Wire plants institutional questions, not personal danger

## Visual Storytelling Requirements

**Show through Action**:
- Emotion → physical action (not description)
- Relationship → gesture/interaction
- Tension → movement/silence

**Dialogue Focus**:
- Target: 40-50% dialogue ratio
- Subtext required: Say X, mean Y
- Interruptions natural
- Cross-talk in groups

**Economical Description**:
- Visual details: functional only
- No decorative prose
- Setting serves story

## Cross-Chapter Threads

**Setup-Payoff Tracking**:
- Plant in chapter N
- Reinforce in chapter N+3
- Payoff in chapter N+M

**Running Threads**:
- Thread A: Episode-to-episode (immediate)
- Thread B: 3-5 episode span (medium)
- Thread C: Season arc (long-term)

## Season Arc Awareness (Novel = Season Model)

When planning chapters, consider season position:

| Chapter Position | Season Function | Pacing Notes |
|------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Chapters 1-2 | Premiere | Setup + inciting + strong hooks |
| Chapters 3-5 | Rising | Stakes clarification + B-story introduction |
| Chapters 6-7 | Pivot | Reversal + revelation + stakes escalate |
| Chapters 8-10 | Climax Build | Convergence + countdown feel |
| Chapters 11-12 | Resolution | Outcome + transformation |
| Chapter 13+ | Finale | Aftermath + seeds + closure |

**Mid-Season Pivot (Chapters 6-7)** is critical:
- Must include reversal or revelation
- Stakes must escalate beyond previous level
- Often changes direction of entire arc
- Example: Breaking Bad S1E7 — Walt's cancer remission traps him in criminal life

## Three-Chapter Summary Injection

Before planning chapter N, consider summary from N-3, N-2, N-1:

**Inherited Elements** (must continue):
- Active conflicts from previous chapters
- Unresolved questions
- Character states
- Running threads

**Adjustable Elements** (can evolve):
- Secondary character positions
- Relationship dynamics
- World expansion

**Warning Markers** (check for conflicts):
- [!] Character mentioned X but not reinforced
- [!] Thread planted but not advanced
- [!] Timeline gaps
